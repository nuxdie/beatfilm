# Three Voids — voiceover reading script

Read it top to bottom in one take, or in as many takes as you like. Each numbered paragraph is one shot.
Leave a clear pause (about a second) between paragraphs; inside a paragraph just read naturally.
If you fluff a line, pause, and read that whole paragraph again; the last complete reading of each paragraph is used.
Quoted labels ("Ask anything.", "Full access"...) get underlined on screen as you say them, so give each a small beat.
Nothing is spoken over the opening (6 s), the title card, or the credits.

**1. [C2]**

A woman stands on a hillside covered in orange poppies. There are mountains behind her, a lake, and tall white clouds. The wind is pulling her dress to the right.

**2. [C3]**

She's holding a palette and a brush. And in front of her, where the rest of the view should be, there's a huge black circle.

**3. [C4]**

The rest of the picture is really detailed, you can make out single petals. The circle is just flat black, like somebody cut a hole in the file. When I first saw it I couldn't tell what she was doing. Is she painting black over a finished world? Or is the world the painting, and the black is the bit she hasn't got to yet?

**4. [P1a]**

The painting is by Daniel Conway, and it's called "Art Block". He made it after losing his creative drive during the pandemic. He says a blank canvas felt like "staring into a void", and that even the world around him had stopped inspiring him.

**5. [P1b]**

So she isn't painting the circle. The circle is the canvas, and she's stuck in front of it. At first I thought the landscape was a bit much. It's postcard pretty. After reading his note it made sense. She's standing in about the most paintable place there is, she has her back to it, and she's got nothing.

**6. [P1c]**

The other thing I noticed is that a blank canvas is white, and he made it black. A white canvas at least looks like you could start on it. This looks like anything you put in would just disappear. It's also kind of funny that the way he got out of the block was to paint the block.

**7. [M2a]**

I should say where I found it, because it wasn't on his page. It was in Minecraft.

**8. [M2b]**

This is the 2b2t museum. If you don't know 2b2t, it's the oldest anarchy server in Minecraft. It's been the same world since December 2010, it has never been reset, and there are basically no rules. You can cheat, you can grief, and nobody stops you. The museum itself is a separate server run by fans, where they keep copies of things people made on 2b2t. A lot of it is map art. You lay out thousands of blocks on the ground so that the in-game map of that area shows a picture.

**9. [M2c]**

Map art only has a small set of colours, so most paintings come out grainy. You can see it on the ship right next to this one. This one holds up well, I think because half of it is one flat black shape and the rest is strong orange and blue.

**10. [M2d]**

And yes, somebody rebuilt a painting called "Art Block" out of blocks. I'd like to think that was on purpose.

**11. [M3a]**

Walking through the archives, I kept seeing two kinds of structures. The first kind is bases. Somebody travelled really far out, built a place to live, and tried to keep it secret. That's a normal Minecraft thing, you'd see the same on any server. The difference is that here a base only lasts until somebody finds it.

**12. [M3b]**

And sooner or later somebody does.

**13. [M3c]**

The second kind is the stuff you don't see anywhere else. Spawn, the highways, giant walls, pyramids, staircases that go on forever.

**14. [M3d]**

Nobody would dig a road hundreds of thousands of blocks long in their own world. People did it here because the only way to keep a base safe is to put it very far away, and then you need some way to get there.

**15. [M3e]**

Spawn is the weirdest one to me, because nobody designed it. It's what's left after sixteen years of people building and blowing things up in the same place. It looks less like something built and more like terrain. The way I put it to myself was this. The first kind is a game with rules being played. The second kind is its players with no rules. Although "no rules" isn't really right. Nobody wrote any down, but if something can be found it gets destroyed, and obsidian is about the only thing that lasts. Everything that's still standing was built with that in mind.

**16. [S4a]**

And at some point I realised I was looking at the painting again.

**17. [S4b]**

The landscape is the bases. It's what you get when you use the thing the normal way. It's well done and nice to look at, and you've seen it before.

**18. [S4c]**

And the black circle is spawn. If you took the circle out of the painting, I honestly wouldn't have stopped to look at it. And 2b2t without the anarchy is a Minecraft server with some nice bases, and there are thousands of those. In both cases the part that looks broken is the reason I care.

**19. [S4d]**

A blank canvas has no rules about what goes on it, and neither does this server. So she's basically a new player who just joined and is standing at spawn.

**20. [M4e]**

The difference is that the painting stops there, with her stuck. On the server people kept going, and what they ended up building in that mess is a lot weirder than any of the bases. So in a way the server shows what comes after the painting, if enough people stay at it for sixteen years.

**21. [U5a]**

Then I closed the game and opened this.

**22. [U5b]**

It's a black screen with a text box in the middle.

**23. [U5c]**

Look at what it says. "Ask anything." "Full access", so no rules here either. "No project", written twice. And then the big one, "What should we work on?", which is exactly the question you can't answer when you're blocked.

**24. [U5d]**

If I put the painting on top, the text box even ends up roughly where she's standing. And this one gives you the least to hold on to. In the painting there's at least a landscape around the hole. Spawn has sixteen years of junk around it. Here there's nothing, so you have to show up with the idea already. The model behind that box can build pretty much any software I can describe. That didn't help me come up with something to describe, the same way the view didn't help her. And it's not just me. A lot of people have already seen this screen, or something close to it. A lot more are about to.

**25. [U6a]**

So I took the lazy option and asked it instead. What software would you build, if you could build anything?

**26. [U6b]**

It answered right away. Its first choice was a tool that traces any claim back to where it came from. The second was a simulator for everyday decisions, like whether to take a job. Both are reasonable ideas. But that's the landscape again. It's the kind of answer you'd expect.

**27. [U6c]**

What got me was the little grey label above the reply: "Deciding how to answer a preferences question." So it didn't really have something it wanted to build. It recognised what kind of question this was and answered that.

**28. [U6d]**

And then the last line asks me what I wish existed. Four paragraphs in, the question is back with me.

**29. [U6e]**

So it has the opposite problem from hers. She can't start. The model starts instantly, every time, and what you get is scenery. I don't know which one is worse.

**30. [U6f]**

But the longer I looked at the first idea, the less generic it seemed. This is a model made entirely out of things other people said. And the thing it wants to build is a tool that shows where the things people say come from. It's describing itself. So I offered it anything, and it picked a self-portrait. Which is what Conway did. The woman in the painting is him.

**31. [E1]**

That's the thing I keep coming back to. Conway got a blank canvas and painted himself standing in front of it. The model got a blank prompt and described a tool for explaining itself. Both of them were handed nothing, and both made a portrait of themselves. And that matters because of what's coming. In the next few months a lot more people on this planet are going to get this tool. I don't mean programmers, I mean everybody. It will build them pretty much anything they can describe. The tool itself, when I asked, wanted a portrait of itself. So what does a human build?

**32. [E2]**

The only hint I have is the server, because it's the one place I know where people could build anything they wanted, for sixteen years. They built roads to get away from each other. They built walls, pyramids, and a crater. They built a wall of shrimp memes. And somebody spent hours copying a painting about having nothing to paint.

**33. [E3]**

I don't have my own answer yet. I sat in front of that box and typed nothing, and then I made this video, which I guess is Conway's trick.

**34. [E4]**

But you're going to be asked the same question soon, if you haven't been already. What would you build?
